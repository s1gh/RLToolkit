# Design Context — RL Toolkit

## Users

Public users of the RL Toolkit project — Rocket League players, streamers,
content creators, plugin authors. They land on the dashboard after installing
the toolkit and want to feel they've picked up something made by someone who
cared. First impressions matter: they may have just come from generic SaaS
admin panels, RGB-laden gaming hardware UIs, or Discord clones. The dashboard
is the front door.

Primary jobs to be done:
- Browse installed plugins and toggle them on/off.
- Open a plugin's interface inline (preview, settings, plugin-specific UI)
  rather than getting kicked into a new browser tab.
- Get to runtime endpoints (`/overlay`, `/events`, `/api/...`) when needed,
  but not have them dominate the page.

Secondary jobs:
- Plugin authors inspecting what's installed and how it's configured.
- Streamers showing the dashboard on a second monitor or via OBS — has to
  read at a glance, no ambiguous chrome.

## Brand Personality

**Three words: warm, opinionated, handcrafted.**

The toolkit is a personal-scale project, not a platform. It should feel like
a tool made by a person with taste, not a product produced by a marketing
team. It can have a point of view. It can be a little quirky. It should never
feel anonymous.

Emotional goals on first launch:
- "Someone cared about this."
- "This isn't trying to look like everything else."
- "I want to read what's in here, not just click through it."

## Aesthetic Direction

**Reference (positive):** Panic Inc. — panic.com, Nova, Transmit. Warm,
confident, tools-for-craftspeople. Strong typography, intentional color,
custom marks, sense of humor without being silly. Reads as opinionated
software made by people who have taste.

**Anti-references (explicitly avoid):**
- Generic SaaS admin (Stripe, Vercel, Linear, Notion). Soft cards on
  near-white, blue accents, evenly-distributed gray text. The "modern
  admin" look has become a monoculture.
- RGB gamer aesthetic (Razer, Logitech, Corsair). Angular, neon,
  carbon-fiber, "competitive edge" energy.
- Discord-style dark UI (gray-purple panels, neon cyan/violet accents,
  gamer-default). The current dashboard skews this direction — the
  redesign moves away from it.
- Corporate / Bootstrap / Material. Sterile, no personality.

**Theme:** Both light and dark with a user toggle (system-preference default).
Both themes share one design language; personality must come from typography,
spacing, content tone, and considered color — not from glow effects that only
work in dark mode.

For light: warm paper tones (off-white tinted toward the brand hue, not pure
white). Ink-toned text. Accents that read as confident, not loud.

For dark: a warm dark — closer to aubergine, iron, or deep brown than to
gray-blue. Cream-toned text. Like a leather notebook under a desk lamp, not
a server-room screen.

**Typography direction (loose, finalized in design phase):**
- Avoid the AI-reflex font monoculture (no Inter, Fraunces, Newsreader,
  IBM Plex, Space Grotesk, DM Sans, Outfit, Crimson, Cormorant, Syne,
  Instrument anything, Plus Jakarta).
- Pair a distinctive display face with a refined body face. Look toward
  type from indie foundries (Pangram Pangram, Klim, ABC Dinamo, Velvetyne)
  or under-used Google Fonts.
- The brief "warm, opinionated, handcrafted" suggests something with
  character — possibly a humanist sans, a slab, or a workhorse serif with
  a strong personality. NOT a "designy" geometric sans.

**Color direction (loose):** OKLCH-based palette built from one warm brand
hue, with neutrals tinted toward that hue (chroma 0.005-0.01 minimum).
Accents are saturated and rare — used for state, not decoration. Pure
white and pure black are forbidden.

## Design Principles

1. **Intentional, not templated.** Every element should feel like a choice.
   If it could be lifted from a starter kit, it doesn't belong here.
2. **Content over chrome.** The plugin list and what plugins say about
   themselves are the page. Topbar, footer, and decorations support that —
   they don't compete with it.
3. **One language across themes.** Whatever makes the dashboard recognizable
   has to survive both light and dark. No glow-driven personality.
4. **Editorial rhythm, not grid uniformity.** Vary spacing for hierarchy.
   Asymmetry is allowed. Resist the auto-fill grid of identical cards
   that defines every modern admin UI.
5. **Quiet authority.** Confidence comes from typography, restraint, and
   considered detail — not from gradients, glows, or copy that performs.
